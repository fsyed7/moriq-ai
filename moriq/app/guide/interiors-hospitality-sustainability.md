# MORIQ AI starter guide: interiors, hospitality and sustainability

AI-written general guidance. Not a MORIQ document or professional advice. Confirm products with suppliers and performance with specialists.

## Natural stone and marble
Marble and limestone are porous, and marble is etched by acids, so think about use in kitchens and bathrooms and specify sealing and care. Inspect and approve slabs, and plan veining and layout before cutting. Allow for movement joints, flatness of the substrate and suitable adhesive, especially on large formats. Check slip resistance for wet areas and outdoor use.

## Timber, veneer and joinery
Timber moves with humidity, so let it acclimatise on site and detail joints for movement. Solid timber suits some uses, while veneer or engineered timber is more stable in air-conditioned rooms. Coordinate joinery with lighting, ventilation, power and appliance sizes. Check shop drawings against site measurements, not only design drawings. Protect exterior timber from sun and water, and plan for maintenance.

## Tiles and wet finishes
Check tile size against the wall layout and set out from centre lines, so cuts fall evenly. Use suitable adhesive, grout and movement joints. Coordinate falls, drains, niches and fittings before tiling, and complete waterproofing tests first. Check slip resistance for bathrooms and pool decks, and order extra from the same batch.

## Lighting design
Layer ambient, task and accent lighting, and use dimming and scenes to change mood. Keep colour temperature consistent within a space, and check colour rendering for stone, timber and art. Coordinate fittings with ceilings, joinery and structure, and allow access to drivers. Control glare from downlights and reflections in polished stone. Mock up important effects.

## Furniture, fabric and procurement
Check sizes against door, lift and stair access for delivery. Imported furniture needs lead time, customs and damage allowance. Choose fabrics for wear, sun fading and cleaning. Keep a schedule with product, supplier, finish, quantity, cost and approval status. Get samples approved in writing before ordering.

## Kitchens and bathrooms
Plan a kitchen around storage, preparation, cooking and cleaning, with ventilation that reaches outside. Show cooking kitchens may be paired with a separate working kitchen in large homes. In bathrooms, coordinate drainage, ventilation and water pressure with chosen fittings before finishing. Check concealed items for access, and confirm that imported fittings suit local plumbing.

## Acoustics in homes
Noise travels through gaps, shared structure and ducts. Separate bedrooms from plant, lifts and home theatres, and seal penetrations. Hard stone and glass make rooms echo, so balance with fabric and absorbent ceilings. Home theatres usually need a specialist. Check equipment noise near neighbours and bedrooms.

## Hotel and hospitality projects
Hotels add operators' brand standards, back-of-house planning, service routes, fire and life-safety strategy and durable finishes. Build a mock-up guest room early, since it repeats many times, so small errors multiply. Separate guest and service circulation. Involve the operator, kitchen and laundry consultants early, and check the approvals that apply locally.

## Commercial and institutional projects
Offices, schools and other public buildings bring stricter occupancy, fire, accessibility and services requirements than homes. Identify the occupancy class and the authority's requirements at the start. Design for adaptable layouts and for operating cost. Check accessibility across the whole route, including entry and toilets.

## Sustainable design priorities
Reduce demand first through orientation, shading, insulation and daylight, then choose efficient systems, then add renewables. Consider rainwater harvesting and wastewater reuse where water is scarce. Prefer durable and local materials, and retain existing trees. Avoid promising performance or a green rating without modelling or a qualified assessor.

## Water, pools and solar
Pools need structure, waterproofing, plant space, filtration, drainage and safety barriers coordinated with a specialist. In hot climates, shade and covers reduce evaporation. Check roof space, structure and shading for solar panels early, and coordinate them with roof waterproofing and appearance. Confirm local rules on water discharge and grid connection.

## Home automation and low-voltage systems
Decide early on lighting control, curtains, security, audio, network and cameras, since cabling and equipment racks must be planned before walls close. Keep a schedule of devices and locations, and provide ventilation and access for the rack. Plan for updates and for manual control if a system fails. Consider client privacy for cameras and data.
